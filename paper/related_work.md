All citation keys below correspond to entries in `refs.bib`. Per-entry verification status is summarized inline; consolidated caveats are listed separately.

## 1. Overcooked benchmark & human data

**Carroll et al. 2019 [carroll2019utility]** — *On the Utility of Learning about Humans for Human-AI Coordination*, NeurIPS 2019 (pp. 5175–5186; arXiv:1910.05789). The canonical citation both for the simplified two-player Overcooked environment ("Overcooked-AI") and for the core motivating observation that self-play agents overfit to self-coordination protocols.
- **Environment**: players cook and serve onion soup (3 onions per pot, 20-timestep cook, plate, serve); 400-timestep episodes; separate agents per layout. **Five layouts** (verified from Fig. 3 caption): Cramped Room (low-level motion coordination), Asymmetric Advantages (strategy choice playing to each player's strengths), Coordination Ring (coordinating travel between corners), Forced Coordination (neither player can serve alone — forces a joint strategy), Counter Circuit (non-obvious counter-passing strategy).
- **Human-human dataset**: collected via web interface on MTurk; after cleaning, 16/17/16/12/15 joint trajectories per layout (~18k joint / ~36k single-agent timesteps per layout). Joint trajectories partitioned into two subsets: behavior cloning on one yields **BC** (the human model given to the agent), on the other **H_proxy** (held-out ground-truth proxy human for evaluation). BC over hand-designed features beat GAIL; a rule-based "get unstuck" mechanism was added.
- **Key finding**: self-play PPO (SP) and PBT excel with themselves but degrade sharply with H_proxy — often failing to match plain BC — while **PPO_BC** (PPO trained against the embedded BC model) does markedly better. A gold-standard PPO trained directly on H_proxy upper-bounds performance; PPO_BC's gap stems from BC's inaccuracy. Same pattern holds for planning, but human-aware planning helps only with an accurate human model (P_BC + H_proxy can get stuck and do worse).
- **User study**: N=60 MTurk participants, between-subjects across SP/PBT/PPO_BC, all 5 layouts. PPO_BC beats self-play methods in 3/5 layouts, on par in 2. ANOVA F(2,224)=6.49, p<.01; Tukey HSD PPO_BC > SP (p=.01), > PBT (p<.01). Qualitative failures of SP/PBT: "headstrong" leader-follower behavior (Coordination Ring), using only one pot (Asymmetric Advantages).
- *Verification*: title/authors/venue/pages verified against arXiv abs page, NeurIPS proceedings + official bibtex, and DBLP; all layout, dataset, agent-lineup, and user-study numbers verified from extracted arXiv PDF text. The "Overcooked-AI" label itself comes from the GitHub repo, not the paper text (repo not fetched). Author-name variants exist across NeurIPS/arXiv/DBLP (e.g., "Tom" vs "Thomas L." Griffiths); the bibtex uses a merged form. A secondary-source claim of "~80% vs 30–40% of gold standard in Cramped Room" was NOT confirmed in paper text and is omitted.

**ZSC-Eval — Wang et al., NeurIPS 2024 D&B [wang2024zsceval]** (arXiv:2310.05208). Argues existing ZSC evaluation is inadequate because evaluation partners are distributionally unlike deployment-time partners. Three components: partner-candidate generation via behavior-preferring rewards, partner selection by Best-Response Diversity (BR-Div), and the Best-Response Proximity (BR-Prox) metric. Benchmarks ZSC algorithms in Overcooked and Google Research Football with a human study checking metric–human agreement. Use for evaluation-protocol justification. *Verified against fetched arXiv abs page (authors, D&B track in comments).*

**OvercookedV2 — Gessler et al., 2025 [gessler2025overcookedv2]** (arXiv:2503.17821). Argues the original Overcooked is too easy as a ZSC benchmark: broad state coverage during training largely solves it, so ZSC failures go untested. Introduces asymmetric information and stochasticity to force coordination strategies beyond exhaustive state coverage. Strong "limitations of the benchmark" citation. *Verified against fetched arXiv abs page; NO venue in the comments field — cite as preprint unless a venue is confirmed.*

(See also Collab-Overcooked in §4, which extends the benchmark toward LLM-agent collaboration.)

## 2. Population-based zero-shot coordination

**Problem framing — Other-Play, Hu et al., ICML 2020 [hu2020otherplay]** (PMLR v119, pp. 4399–4410; arXiv:2003.02979). Shows vanilla self-play converges to arbitrary conventions that fail with novel partners, and proposes breaking known game symmetries to train agents robust to unseen partners. The standard citation for the zero-shot coordination (ZSC) problem framing that motivates population-based training. *Fully verified against both arXiv and PMLR pages.*

**TrajeDi — Lupu et al., ICML 2021 [lupu2021trajectory]** (PMLR v139, pp. 7204–7213). Originated the diversity-regularized-population + common-best-response recipe: a differentiable diversity objective derived as a generalization of Jensen-Shannon divergence over trajectory distributions, with a common BR trained against the diverse population. Demonstrated in a matrix game and **Hanabi** (not Overcooked). *Verified against the official PMLR page. Note: no arXiv abs page exists (a search hit, arXiv:2106.02912, is an unrelated physics paper); an AAMAS 2021 extended abstract also exists.*

**FCP — Strouse et al., NeurIPS 2021 spotlight [strouse2021collaborating]** (arXiv:2110.08176). The canonical population-based / partner-diversity baseline for zero-shot human-AI coordination **without any human data**: train a best response to a population of self-play agents *and their past checkpoints* — checkpoints supply skill-level diversity, multiple runs supply convention diversity. Outperforms SP, population play (PP), and BC baselines with novel partners and real humans in a collaborative cooking simulator (Overcooked); human participants also expressed a strong subjective preference for FCP. Typically contrasted with BC-based approaches (Carroll et al.'s PPO_BC) that require human gameplay data. *Verified against arXiv abs + NeurIPS proceedings pages. Page numbers (commonly 14502–14515) NOT verified and omitted; "Overcooked" branding in paper body not confirmed from fetched pages (abstract says "collaborative cooking simulator").*

**MEP — Zhao et al., AAAI 2023 [zhao2023maximum]** (arXiv:2112.11701; AAAI vol. 37(5), pp. 6145–6153, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v37i5.25758). Two stages: (1) train a population with a derived **Population Entropy bonus** promoting both pairwise and within-agent diversity; (2) train a common best-response via **prioritized sampling** of population partners, prioritization adjusted with training progress. Evaluated in Overcooked against SP, PBT, TrajeDi, and FCP, with human-proxy models and real humans. Framing note (synthesis): MEP adopts TrajeDi's population+BR structure but swaps trajectory-JSD for a population-entropy bonus, adds prioritized sampling, and evaluates in Overcooked with humans. *Verified against arXiv abs + AAAI OJS pages; arXiv comments field is stale ("under review") — AAAI page is authoritative for venue fields; AAAI name forms used for two authors arXiv lists surname-first.*

**HSP — Yu et al., ICLR 2023 [yu2023hsp]** (arXiv:2302.01605). Verified title is *"Learning Zero-Shot Cooperation with Humans, Assuming Humans Are Biased"* (HSP = Hidden-utility Self-Play is the method name, not the title). Models humans as **biased** — holding hidden utility functions distinct from the task reward — and augments the task reward with a linear combination of event-based features to build a policy pool with genuinely different *preferences* (not just skill levels), then trains an ego agent against the pool. Evaluated in Overcooked. Good citation for "population diversity must span reward/preference space, not just behavior space." *Verified against fetched arXiv abs page (ICLR 2023 in comments; first two authors equal contribution).*

**E3T — Yan et al., NeurIPS 2023 [yan2023e3t]**. The efficiency foil for population-based methods: argues two-stage population-based ZSC is computationally expensive, and removes the population entirely — the partner is a **mixture of the ego policy and a random policy**, trained end-to-end, plus a **partner modeling module** predicting partner actions from history. Comparable-or-better Overcooked performance at much lower cost. *Verified against NeurIPS 2023 proceedings page and OpenReview (id 6ePsuwXUwf). **No arXiv version exists** — three separate arXiv API queries returned zero hits; cite the proceedings only.*

**GAMMA — Liang et al., NeurIPS 2024 [liang2024gamma]** (arXiv:2411.13934). The most direct 2024 successor to population-based ZSC: replaces a fixed discrete population with a **generative model over partners** (latent-variable strategy/style representation); sampling the latent space yields unlimited diverse training partners. Adds cheap biasing of latent sampling with a small amount of real human data; consistent improvement with real human teammates whether fit on simulated populations or human data. *Verified against arXiv abs + NeurIPS 2024 poster page (which confirms the venue; the arXiv abs has no venue comment). Name variant: arXiv "Simon S. Du" vs NeurIPS "Simon Du" — bibtex uses arXiv form.*

**UED alternative — You et al., IEEE Access 2025 [you2025curriculum]** (arXiv:2503.07275; IEEE Access 13:143606–143617). Extends multi-agent **unsupervised environment design** to human coordination via a modified utility function and co-player sampling — i.e., diversity over *environments* rather than partner populations. Evaluated in Overcooked-AI with proxy agents and real humans. Position as the UED alternative to population diversity (the abstract does not frame itself as population-based). *Verified against fetched arXiv abs page including the journal reference.*

**Adjacent population-based work (venues NOT verified)**: **PECAN [lou2023pecan]** (policy ensemble + context-awareness; arXiv:2301.06387, commonly cited as AAMAS 2023) and **COLE-line "Tackling Cooperative Incompatibility" [li2023cooperative]** (arXiv:2306.03034, commonly cited as ICML 2023). Both verified only via arXiv API metadata (title/authors/date); both given as arXiv preprints in the bib — confirm venues before switching to conference forms.

## 3. MARL foundations

**PPO — Schulman et al. 2017 [schulman2017proximal]** (arXiv:1707.06347). Clipped surrogate objective; the single-agent RL backbone of population-based Overcooked methods. **arXiv-only** — never published at a peer-reviewed venue, so cite as preprint. *Verified against fetched arXiv abs page (no venue listed).*

**GAE — Schulman et al., ICLR 2016 [schulman2016gae]** (arXiv:1506.02438, first posted June 2015). Lambda-weighted advantage estimator used in essentially every PPO implementation. Conventional citation is 2016 (ICLR), not the 2015 preprint. *arXiv page fetched; the ICLR 2016 venue came from a DBLP mention surfaced in that fetch, not a directly fetched ICLR/DBLP page — the exact booktitle phrasing is the researcher's.*

**MAPPO — Yu et al., NeurIPS 2022 D&B [yu2022surprising]** (arXiv:2103.01955). PPO with centralized value functions matches or beats off-policy MARL on MPE, SMAC, Hanabi, GRF with minimal tuning; the standard citation for (MA)PPO in cooperative MARL. Title gotcha: the arXiv title has a comma ("Cooperative, Multi-Agent Games"); the official proceedings title does not — use the proceedings title (as the bibtex does). *arXiv page fetched (acceptance in comments); comma-free title confirmed via proceedings search-result title, page not fetched.*

**Behavior cloning origins** — two standard citations: **Pomerleau 1991 [pomerleau1991efficient]** (Neural Computation 3(1):88–97; ALVINN driving from human demonstrations — the canonical early BC citation) and **Bain & Sammut 1995 [bain1995framework]** (Machine Intelligence 15, pp. 103–129 — coined "behavioural cloning"). *Pomerleau verified via Semantic Scholar API record (DOI 10.1162/neco.1991.3.1.88); issue number corroborated from DOI/PDF path only. Bain & Sammut verified via fetched DBLP record; publisher "Oxford University Press" from search snippets only. The 1989 NIPS ALVINN paper and the claim that the MI15 book volume appeared in 1999 are from memory, unverified.*

## 4. Human-AI teaming & LLM agent collaboration

**Mixed-initiative interaction — Horvitz, CHI 1999 [horvitz1999mixedinitiative]** (pp. 159–166, DOI 10.1145/302979.303030). Foundational principles for coupling automated services with direct manipulation: reason under uncertainty about user goals; weigh expected utility of autonomous action vs. dialog; graceful degradation; preserve user control; learn from interaction (LookOut system). The 25-year-old HCI lineage a multiplayer-workspace motivation can anchor to before modern LLM agents. *Title/author/venue verified from erichorvitz.com; pages/DOI rest on ACM PDF listing + secondary sources (ACM DL landing page returned 403).*

**NAS consensus report 2022 [nationalacademies2022humanaiteaming]** — *Human-AI Teaming: State-of-the-Art and Research Needs* (committee chaired by Mica R. Endsley; DOI 10.17226/26355). Research agenda for AI as *teammate* rather than tool (situation awareness, trust, transparency, HSI). Finds AI suffers "brittleness, perceptual limitations, hidden biases, and lack of a model of causation" in complex settings; argues the unit of evaluation should be combined human-AI team effectiveness, not standalone capability — directly motivates evaluating agents *with* humans rather than in self-play. *Verified from nationalacademies.org publication page (title, DOI, ISBN, chair, themes). Only the 10-digit paperback ISBN appeared on the fetched page.*

**Multi-agent LLM frameworks** — **AutoGen [wu2024autogen]** (COLM 2024; arXiv:2308.08155): LLM applications as multi-agent conversations with human input as a first-class participation mode. **CAMEL [li2023camel]** (NeurIPS 2023; arXiv:2303.17760): role-playing + inception prompting for autonomous cooperation between communicative agents. **MetaGPT [hong2024metagpt]** (ICLR 2024; arXiv:2308.00352): encodes human SOPs into prompt sequences, assembly-line roles with intermediate verification. **Framing hook**: these frameworks orchestrate agent-agent collaboration but largely sidestep *adaptation-to-humans* — the human is an input source, not a partner the agents must model — precisely the gap population-based RL for human-AI coordination addresses. *AutoGen verified via arXiv + Microsoft Research page (COLM 2024, Best Paper at LLM Agents Workshop @ ICLR'24); COLM camera-ready author list unchecked (OpenReview blocked) — arXiv v2's fuller list kept. CAMEL verified via arXiv (NeurIPS'23 acceptance in comments); volume/pages unverified. MetaGPT verified via arXiv + DBLP; DBLP lists "Jonathan Chen" where arXiv says "Jiaqi Chen" (bibtex follows DBLP/ICLR).*

**LLM agents in Overcooked** — **ProAgent [zhang2024proagent]** (AAAI 2024; arXiv:2308.11339, 15 authors): LLM-based proactive cooperator that infers teammate intent, updates beliefs from observed actions, and adapts plans; reports >10% average improvement over then-SOTA with human proxy models — a useful LLM-vs-population-RL comparison point. **HLA [liu2024hla]** (AAMAS 2024; arXiv:2312.15224; author group overlaps heavily with HSP): three-tier architecture — Slow Mind (large LLM, intention reasoning + dialogue), Fast Mind (lightweight LLM, macro-actions), Executor (reactive policy, atomic actions) — trading reasoning depth against latency in Overcooked with natural-language communication; the pitch is real-time responsiveness monolithic LLM agents cannot hit. **Collab-Overcooked [sun2025cooverc]** (EMNLP 2025 Main; arXiv:2502.20073; ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-main.249): LLM-multi-agent benchmark on Overcooked-AI with 30 open-ended tasks, natural-language collaboration, and process-oriented (not just outcome) metrics; evaluates 13 LLMs — headline finding is that models understand goals but "exhibit significant shortcomings in active collaboration and continuous adaptation." *All three verified against fetched arXiv abs pages (venue in comments fields); page numbers omitted throughout — not fetched from sources stating ranges.*
